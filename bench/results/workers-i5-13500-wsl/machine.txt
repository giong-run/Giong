Things that affect how to read the numbers:
- WSL2 does not pin vCPUs to physical cores. Windows schedules them over the
  P-cores and E-cores as it likes, and Windows 10 does not know the hybrid
  layout well. The 1-worker results look too low (2 workers give 2.1x to 2.5x
  the throughput for four servers), so the "eff." column means little here.
- At 10 workers the server and wrk keep 20 vCPUs busy on 14 physical cores.
- The guest's clock jumped when systemd-timesyncd and WSL's own time sync
  disagreed (the Windows clock was about 72 s fast), which broke wrk's timing
  in a first attempt (thrown away). systemd-timesyncd was disabled in the
  guest; afterwards no clock step above 0.5 ms was seen in 3 minutes of
  watching, and wrk's 6 s runs measured 6.10 s.
